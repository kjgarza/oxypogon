## Workout Overview

Hey team! Today’s WOD is all about quality and consistency. We’re diving into a 6-round workout that combines some great cardio, strength, and bodyweight movements. The goal here is to maintain good form throughout each round while focusing on your breathing and pacing. We want to build endurance and strength without sacrificing technique. Let’s break it down!

## Workout Components

### 1. Row/Ski Erg (3:00)

- **Technique Steps:**
    - For the Row: Sit on the rower with your feet securely strapped in. Grab the handle with both hands, keeping your back straight and core engaged.
    - Push through your legs first, then lean back slightly as you pull the handle towards your chest. Reverse the motion to return to the starting position.
    - For the Ski Erg: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, grasp the handles with both hands. Initiate the movement by pulling down with your arms while bending your knees slightly, then return to the starting position.
  
- **Form Tips:**
    - Keep your back straight and avoid rounding your shoulders.
    - Use your legs to drive the movement, not just your arms.
  
- **Scaling/Modifications:**
    - If you’re new to rowing or skiing, reduce the time to 2:00 or even 1:30 to focus on form.
    - You can also switch to a stationary bike if available.

### 2. Box Step-Ups (20 reps)

- **Technique Steps:**
    - Stand facing the box with feet hip-width apart. Step up with one foot, driving through your heel to lift your body onto the box.
    - Bring your other foot up to meet the first, then step back down one foot at a time.
  
- **Form Tips:**
    - Keep your chest up and engage your core throughout the movement.
    - Make sure your entire foot is on the box before standing up.
  
- **Scaling/Modifications:**
    - Use a lower box if you’re not comfortable with the height.
    - You can also perform step-ups without weights to focus on balance and form.

### 3. Kettlebell Sumo Deadlift High Pulls (15 reps)

- **Technique Steps:**
    - Stand with your feet wider than shoulder-width apart, toes slightly pointed out. Place the kettlebell between your feet.
    - Hinge at the hips to grab the kettlebell with both hands. Keep your back flat and chest up.
    - Drive through your heels to stand up, pulling the kettlebell up towards your chin, keeping your elbows high.
  
- **Form Tips:**
    - Maintain a flat back and avoid rounding your shoulders.
    - Keep the kettlebell close to your body as you pull it up.
  
- **Scaling/Modifications:**
    - Use a lighter kettlebell if you’re new to this movement.
    - If kettlebells aren’t available, you can substitute with dumbbells or perform a bodyweight squat.

### 4. Ring Rows (10 reps)

- **Technique Steps:**
    - Set the rings at a height where you can comfortably reach them while lying on your back. Grab the rings with an overhand grip.
    - Lean back, keeping your body straight, and pull your chest towards the rings, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
  
- **Form Tips:**
    - Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels.
    - Avoid letting your hips sag or your shoulders round forward.
  
- **Scaling/Modifications:**
    - Adjust the height of the rings to make the movement easier or harder.
    - If rings are not available, you can use a barbell set at waist height for inverted rows.

## Loading and Rep-Modification Options

- **Sweat:** 
    - Use a lighter kettlebell (16/8 kg) and focus on form and technique. 
    - Consider reducing the number of rounds to 4 if you’re feeling fatigued.

- **Train:** 
    - Use a moderate kettlebell (20/12 kg) and aim for all 6 rounds. 
    - Maintain a steady pace and focus on quality over quantity.

- **Compete:** 
    - Use a heavier kettlebell (24/16 kg) and push for intensity while keeping form sharp. 
    - Challenge yourself to complete all rounds with minimal rest.

### Strategy Advice

- Focus on maintaining a consistent pace throughout the workout. It’s better to go slower and keep your form than to rush and risk injury.
- Break up the movements into manageable sets if you start to feel fatigued, especially on the kettlebell high pulls and ring rows.
- Remember to breathe! Use your breath to help maintain your rhythm and keep your heart rate in check.
